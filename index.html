WATTO’CLOCK

The greenest half-hours to use electricity — from NESO’s live carbon forecast for your bit of the grid.

This preview is blocking internet access, so live NESO data can’t load here. Download watt-oclock.html from the chat and open it directly in a browser — double-clicking the file on a computer is enough, no server needed. Everything in the app works from a plain local file.

Every unit of electricity you use has a carbon cost, and it changes through the day — low when wind and solar are plentiful, high when gas plants pick up the slack.

Enter your postcode and Watt O’Clock reads the next 24 hours of NESO’s half-hourly forecast for your region, then finds the cleanest start time for each appliance — weighted by when it actually draws power, not just how long it runs.

When to press start

Tap an appliance, then set its run time to match your programme — an eco dishwash might take 3½ h. The heating bursts stay anchored to the start and end of the cycle; only the quiet middle stretches. Darker green on the chart means heavier draw.

What’s on the wires right now

Forecast data from the NESO Carbon Intensity API (licence CC BY 4.0). Regional forecasts are half-hourly, indicative, and cover Great Britain only — Northern Ireland is on a separate grid.

Times are shown in your local timezone. Forecasts refresh every 30 minutes, so check back before you press start. The “index” badge compares your region to typical national levels; bar colours compare each half-hour to the rest of your day. Appliance profiles and kWh figures are typical shapes (a 40° wash, a standard dishwasher cycle, a 7 kW charger) — your machine will differ in detail, but the timing logic holds. The kWh estimate scales with your chosen run time and is editable; eco programmes often use less energy than the estimate despite running longer, so type in your machine’s figure if you know it. Changing the run time re-estimates the energy.
